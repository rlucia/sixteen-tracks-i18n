Before you start

Two ideas every session leans on, a map of what a save saves, and three things to do before session 1. Ten minutes.

Four kinds of track

The bottom row of the panel is sixteen keys, and the labels under them say what the sixteen sequencer tracks are (§5.3):

  • Audio, 1–8. One preset apiece, a pair of insert effects of its own, and a send level aimed at each of the three send tracks. For movement: two LFOs on the voice, two more on the effects, and one extra modulation envelope. Give the track the MIDI machine instead and it drives another instrument rather than making a sound itself.
  • Bus, 9–12. Somewhere to point several tracks at once so they can be treated as one, and it too has a pair of insert effects. A bus will take the MIDI machine as well.
  • Send FX, 13–15. One effect apiece; what reaches them is whatever the other tracks send.
  • Mix, 16. Where the levels of every track meet the audio inputs and the pattern's main effect.

Hold TRK and tap a trig key: the track is selected and stays quiet (§5.3.7). Tap the same key on its own and you select it and hear it. Move to any track from 9 to 16, though, and the keyboard stays with the audio track you were on before. Play it and the old track answers — not silence, which is what makes it confusing, and the likeliest reason a note seems to come from the wrong track.

Five containers

Where a thing lives decides when it is safe. From the outside in (§5.2):

  1. The SD card. Everything, permanently. Projects, presets, instruments, samples, settings.
  2. The project. 128 patterns, 16 songs, the sample and wavetable pools, its settings. The active project is what the machine boots into.
  3. The pattern. Trigs, locks, length, tempo, swing, time signature — and the presets of its twelve tracks.
  4. The preset. A track's parameter pages, its setup, its arpeggiator. When you load one, the pattern gets its own copy (§9).
  5. The pools. Samples and wavetables held in RAM: 1023 sample slots and 127 wavetable slots (§5.2.6), the samples coming to 4 GB at most (§6.13).

The consequences to hold on to: a knob turn changes the pattern's copy of the preset; a pattern is saved only when the project is saved; chains are never saved (§10.1.3); PERFORM mode (§10.12) and the temporary memorise (§10.10.6) are not saves; songs need the project saved too (§10.11.3).

Update to OS 1.4.0

This site describes OS 1.4.0 and nothing else. Check yours: SETTINGS › SYSTEM › DEVICE INFO. To update (§6.12): download the OS file from Elektron's support pages, connect the Tonverk to a computer, SETTINGS › USB DISK MODE › YES, copy the OS file to the root of the card, eject, NO to leave disk mode, then SETTINGS › SYSTEM › OS UPGRADE › YES.

Back up first

Before session 1, copy the card's USER folder to a computer the same way (§6.11): USB DISK MODE, copy the folder, eject, NO. Everything you make — projects, presets, instruments, recordings, added samples — lives in that folder. A backup is what makes every experiment free.
